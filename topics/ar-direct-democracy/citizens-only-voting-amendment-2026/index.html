The Citizens Only Voting Amendment (HJR 1018 of 2025) — Overview

Page last updated October 4, 2026 · Analysis validated October 4, 2026 · Sources last checked September 9, 2026

Issue 1 — The Citizens Only Voting Amendment

What it is

→ Issue 1 would change the Arkansas Constitution. Voters decide it on November 3, 2026.

How it reached the ballot

→ The General Assembly put it there. It did not come from a citizen petition. Lawmakers proposed it in 2025 as House Joint Resolution 1018. Representative Ray was the lead sponsor. Article 19, Section 22 of the state constitution lets the legislature refer amendments this way. The Secretary of State then assigned it a number, and it appears on the ballot as Issue No. 1.

What it is called

→ The resolution gives itself a short title: The Citizens Only Voting Amendment. That is the name most people will hear. Its popular name on the ballot is longer. The resolution sets that too, in full: A Constitutional Amendment to be Known as "The Citizens Only Voting Amendment" and Providing That Only a Citizen of the United States Meeting the Qualifications of an Elector Under the Arkansas Constitution May Vote in an Election in this State.

What would change

→ Under the Arkansas Constitution today, Article 3, Section 1 (who may vote) lists the qualifications a voter must have. Being a citizen of the United States is the first of them. That requirement is already in the constitution, not only in state law.

→ This amendment would add two new subsections above that list. The first says that ONLY a citizen of the United States who meets those qualifications may vote in an election in this state. The second says a person who does not meet them shall not be permitted to vote in any state or local election held in this state.

→ So the change is not the citizenship requirement itself. It is the word "only," and an express prohibition that reaches local elections as well as state ones.

→ The amendment also rewords the opening of the list. Today it says "any person may vote in an election in this state who" meets the list. The new text would say "to vote in an election in this state a person shall be" as listed.

What changes, and what only gets renumbered

→ The amendment reprints all of Article 3, Section 1 (who may vote). It has to. That is how a section of the constitution gets changed.

→ So the full text shows language about free photographic identification. It also shows language about provisional ballots. A reader may think the measure adds those things.

→ It does not. Both are in the constitution now. ARvoters checked the new text against its own copy of the current constitution. The words match exactly. Only the subsection letters move down.

→ The real change is the two new subsections above them.

When it would take effect

→ A majority of the votes cast on the measure decides it. If it passes, the amendment starts on January 1, 2027.

Where to read it yourself

→ The Secretary of State publishes the ballot notice for Issue No. 1 — three pages: the popular name, the ballot title and the full text of the amendment. ARvoters holds a copy of that document. On September 9, 2026, we checked that our copy matches the one the State published.

Questions for discussion

  1. Citizenship is already required to register to vote in Arkansas. What changes in practice if the same rule moves into the constitution?
  2. Who checks whether a voter meets the citizenship requirement now, and does this measure change that process or only where the rule is written?
  3. What would it take to undo or adjust this provision later if it caused problems?
  4. This measure came from the legislature, not from a petition. Should a voter weigh a referred measure any differently?
  5. The two new subsections reach local elections as well as state ones. Which elections does that cover, and who runs them?
